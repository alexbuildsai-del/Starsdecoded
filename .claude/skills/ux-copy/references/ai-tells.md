# AI tells: habits that make copy read as machine-made

The scan for the conversational pass, strongest first. Sections A and E justify an
edit on one sighting. The rest are *weak alone*: act when two or more share a passage,
or when one repeats across the page. Every example is from our own drafts or the
product. Adapted from humanizer (MIT, github.com/blader/humanizer), itself based on
Wikipedia's "Signs of AI writing".

## A. Staging instead of saying

1. **The "noun, phrase" tagline.** A noun, a comma, then a participle or a phrase:
   "Your birth chart, explained in plain words." "Three steps, from your birth to your
   report." Every landing page opens this way. Say the sentence you would say: "Find out
   what your birth chart says about you". Allowed once per page at most, on purpose.
2. **Not X but Y.** "Not a horoscope, but a report." "Real life, not horoscopes." The
   negative half answers a claim nobody made. Say Y. Keep a contrast only when the
   reader really believes X.
3. **Rows of fragments and one-line closers.** "No scores. No fluff. Just you." "That's
   the difference." Merge into one sentence that carries a fact: "It never gives you a
   score."
4. **Sayings that sound deep.** "Your sky, read closely." "Everyone you read joins your
   sky." "The stars don't decide. You do." An ordinary point dressed as an aphorism.
   Replace it with the plain claim: "Add the people you care about".
5. **Staged run-ups.** "Here's the thing." "Let's be honest." "Here's what you need to
   know." Cut the run-up and start with the point.

## B. Rhythm by rule

6. **Forced triads.** "Innovation, insight and inspiration." Three items because three
   sounds complete. Keep three only when there are three real things: "how you think,
   work and love" names three chapters, so it stays.
7. **Dashes as the connector.** Em and en dashes between clauses. We use none; pick a
   full stop, a comma, a colon or brackets. A product string we quote keeps its dash.
8. **Question and short answer.** "Worried about your birth time? Don't be." Answer in
   one sentence: "You still get the full report without a birth time."
9. **Stacked qualifiers.** "May potentially help you possibly understand." Keep one
   hedge where the report is really uncertain ("tends to"), none elsewhere.
10. **Hidden subjects.** "Positions computed. Houses assigned." Say who does it: "We work
    out your chart".

## C. Inflation

11. **Stock AI words.** Unlock, unleash, elevate, empower, seamless, journey, delve,
    dive into, discover your true self, navigate, landscape, tapestry, testament,
    profound, transformative, cosmic blueprint, crucial, key (adjective), robust.
12. **Inflated significance.** "A pivotal moment in your self-discovery." Drop the
    significance and keep the fact.
13. **Sales language.** Stunning, breathtaking, powerful, revolutionary, like never
    before. State what the thing is and does.
14. **Borrowed authority.** "Experts agree", "astrologers love it", invented numbers of
    readers. Name a real source or cut the claim. No fake counters or reviews.
15. **Avoiding is and has.** "Serves as", "stands as", "features", "boasts". Use is, are,
    has.

## D. Formatting by rule

16. **Title Case.** "Get Your Report". Sentence case everywhere: "Get my report".
    Product names keep their capitals: Personal natal report, Compatibility report.
17. **Bold labels on every item** and **decorative icons or emoji** before headings. A
    label earns its bold only when readers scan for it.
18. **Heading repeated in the first line.** "Privacy. Your privacy matters to us." Start
    with the content.

## E. Interface leftovers

19. **Apology with no fact.** "Oops! Something went wrong." Say what happened and what
    to do: "Our writing service didn't answer. Try again in a few minutes."
20. **Generic closers.** "You're all set!" "Enjoy!" End on the next step or on nothing.
21. **Unnamed consequence.** "Are you sure?" Say what goes: "Delete Mira's report? Her
    birth details go too, unless another report uses them."
22. **Chat residue.** "Great question!" "I hope this helps." Remove the wrapper.

## When not to act

A person can choose any one of these on purpose, which is why weak tells need company.
Leave alone: text inside quotation marks, report text (quoted, never edited), product
names and chapter titles from the code, legal text, and labels that are labels.
"No predictions" and "Start with your birth date." are fine as they are; not every line
has to be a sentence. Never add a fact, number or name to make a rewrite work: if the
fix needs a fact you don't have, ask for it or write the simpler sentence.
