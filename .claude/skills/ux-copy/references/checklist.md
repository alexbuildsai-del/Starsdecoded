# Checklist: score a string, a page or a flow

Score each standard 0 to 10 before and after; report both. Adapted from the content
usability checklist in content-designer/ux-writing-skill (MIT).

**Purposeful.** It helps the reader do what they came for. The value comes before the
ask. It's about them ("you"), not about us. On a public page, the lede answers the
page's question and names Stars Decoded.

**Concise.** Every word has a job. The point or the action comes first. No filler, no
repeated idea. Sentences 15 words on average, none over 25; buttons two to four words.

**Conversational.** You'd say it out loud. Active voice, small words kept. No system
speak, no AI tells (`ai-tells.md`), no slang. Personality only where the moment allows.

**Clear.** Plain words, grade 7 to 8. Specific verbs. One name per thing, the names in
`product.ts`. Numbers from code. No meaning by colour alone; link text stands alone.

**House checks** (pass or fail): both reports named where either could be meant; no
model or vendor; AI never leads; no predictions, dates, fate or hype; report text
untouched; no em dashes, semicolons, exclamation marks or emoji.

**Scores.** 9 to 10 ship. 7 to 8 small fixes. 5 to 6 rework. Below 5 rewrite.

## Example

"Everyone you read joins your sky." (draft 3, people section heading)
- Purposeful 4: it doesn't say what you can do.
- Concise 7: short, but the words don't carry the point.
- Conversational 2: nobody talks like this; you don't "read" people.
- Clear 3: "joins your sky" means nothing without the product in front of you.

"Add the people you care about"
- Purposeful 9: says the action.
- Concise 9.
- Conversational 9: what you'd say.
- Clear 9: plain words, and the sub names who (partner, parents, kids, friends).
