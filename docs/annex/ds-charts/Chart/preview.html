The chart

We have one chart. It is drawn by one function, from one set of measurements, and it is never drawn a second way. Every screen uses this same chart in a state: some layers on, some off, one thing lit. This page shows what the chart is made of, how each part is drawn, and the order it is drawn in. The loading story draws it in that same order.

The parts

Thibault's real chart (born 18 May 1993, Brussels), as the engine computes it. Each number points at one part. Each part has its own page.

  1. Sign ring. Twelve equal slices of 30°, one per sign. Grey, two shades taking turns. Sign ring page.
  2. Sign names. Written along the ring, never upside down. Sign ring page.
  3. House words. "7 · PARTNERSHIP": the house number and its one word. Houses page.
  4. Degree ticks. A short tick every 5°, a longer one every 30°. Sign ring page.
  5. Houses. Twelve slices inside the ticks. House 1 is the rising sign. Houses page.
  6. Planets. The ten renders, plus Chiron and the two nodes as drawn points. Planets page.
  7. Lines between planets. Inside the small circle. Blue is easy, red is tense, brass is joined. Lines page.
  8. Horizon. A flat line. Left is east, where the sky rises. Horizon page.
  9. Rising marker. The ring with a dot, at 9 o'clock: the rising degree. Horizon page.
  10. Going backwards. A small red R beside a planet. Planets page.

How it is built, step by step

This is the order the chart is drawn in, everywhere it is drawn in motion: the loading story, the landing page, a chart's first appearance. The steps come from how a chart really works. First the sky of the day, then the birth time turns it, then the houses are counted.

  1. The sign ring. The twelve slices appear one by one, Aries first, going round counter-clockwise. Aries starts at 9 o'clock, because we don't know the rising sign yet.
  2. Names and ticks. The sign names, the two circles and the degree ticks fade in.
  3. The planets run to their degrees. Each one starts at 0° Aries and runs along its lane to where it stood. Slowest to arrive: the ones furthest round.
  4. The lines between planets. Tightest first, so the strongest ones are drawn first.
  5. The birth time turns the sky. The whole sky turns until the rising degree sits at 9 o'clock, on the left end of the horizon.
  6. The horizon and the rising marker. The flat line draws, and the marker drops onto the rising degree.
  7. The houses, two at a time. Opposite houses light together, 1 and 7, then 2 and 8, up to 6 and 12. Each one's word stays.

The measurements

Every size is a share of the chart's width, S. They live in one place, wheelRadii() in web/src/components/chart/wheel-geometry.ts. A chart at any size is the same chart, scaled. Nothing on this page adds a radius.

Framing: a longitude l is drawn at angle 180° + (l − rising degree), counted counter-clockwise from 3 o'clock. So the rising degree is always at 9 o'clock, and the signs run counter-clockwise from it (theta()).

Do

  • Use this chart for every natal chart in the product. Pick a state.
  • Take every size from wheelRadii(S).
  • Draw it in motion in the build order above, and only in that order.
  • Keep the rising degree at 9 o'clock whenever the birth time is known.
  • Use real chart data, computed by the engine at run time.

Don't

  • Don't draw a second chart with its own radii, dots or colours. That is what we are removing.
  • Don't add an MC line. It isn't explained anywhere, so it is gone.
  • Don't use brass for the frame. Brass means "lit".
  • Don't use dots for planets. Planets are the renders.
  • Don't make up a placement, even in a demo.
