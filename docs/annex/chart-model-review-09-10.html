Part 2 · the chart system

One chart, drawn one way, everywhere

We draw a chart in about fifteen places, and they've drifted: dots in the loading story, symbols in the ledger, long lines through the small pair charts. The model is the wheel on this page, with every decision of this session. It started from the landing page's wheel; now it leads. Every chart takes its format, the landing page's included, in a few states, with the rules written down.

  1. The horizon is flat. The rising degree sits at 9 o'clock, level, on every chart, with the Ascendant's one marker; the chart turns under it. The signs run counter-clockwise. No MC line: it appears only beside the sentence that explains it.
  2. Two lines on the rim, curved with the circle: the sign outside, the house inside ("4 · HOME"). Text follows the ring like a clock face, never straight across it.
  3. The ticks, every 5°, longer every 30°.
  4. The bodies are our planet pictures at their true degree. When two crowd, one steps inward, never sideways. On a report chart, Chiron and the nodes are always there.
  5. The aspect lines, exactly as the site draws them: inside the inner circle, the engine's list, fainter when wide, dashed when separating. Only on the full chart; How two planets meet explains them.

One palette rule, for every chart, the landing page too

The landing page todayAll brass, with the MC line. It changes to the model; its motion (the first light, the rewind) stays.

Seven states of the same chart

Do
  • Draw from the engine's numbers, never by hand.
  • Use the planet pictures for bodies, at every size.
  • Draw the frame in grey-blue; keep brass for what's lit, the part the words are about.
  • Dim what's not the point instead of removing it.
  • At most three colours a screen: the chapter's colour, the one the content carries (teal or rose for a link, brass for a lit chart part), and greys. Contrast by size, type and grey, not by a new hue.
  • Reuse a live part's words, colours and order before making one: a new tag, label or icon goes in this page first.
  • Let a loading story build the chart in this same geometry, step by step.
Don't
  • Swap a planet for a dot or a symbol (the loading story, the ledger do today).
  • Draw the axes on a small chart (pair plates, the hero): there they read as lines through it.
  • Join two people's charts with a line between them (ADR-97).
  • Write rim text straight across the circle, or let it spill outside the ring.
  • Draw the MC, or any line, that nothing on the screen explains.
  • Show the aspect lines on a teaching chart, unless the card is about that line.
  • Move a planet off its true degree to make room.

Why the line went

There's no single strongest link. The brief sorts links by how close to exact they are, and I drew the closest one (Beatrice's Jupiter and Athena's Sun, 0.0° apart). But a pair has a dozen links, and an old decision (ADR-97) says two charts are never joined by a line. So the hero shows the two people, and the links come in the walk.

Names with numbers: today a name takes letters only (the name rule, ADR-202), so "Alexandra 9/10" is refused. Names now reach the writer as plain data, so digits are safe to allow. Question 1 below.

How two planets meet

We never explained this. A link is the angle between two planets, one from each chart, measured round the zodiac. A few angles count. You loved this one, so it's saved as a model visual, and it goes on the site (the Learn pages) and behind each link's ⓘ.

The walk: each link shown on the two charts

No third circle. On your chart, your planet lights up with a solid ring, and Athena's planet sits on it as a faint guest with a dashed ring, at its true degree. The angle between them is drawn across your chart's centre, where a chart draws its aspects, so a square looks like a square. Athena's chart shows the same from her side. Inside the circle, the link's whole shape is drawn faintly (a triangle for a trine, a square for a square, a hexagon for a sextile), with the pair's own side bright. Each card is as tall as its words; the page settles on the card you stop near; the charts show the group, the link number and how many are left.
